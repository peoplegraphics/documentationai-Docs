Exports produce **offline versions** of your work for print, pitching, and social media. They are separate from [publishing](/cms/publishing): publishing puts the interactive work online for readers, while an export renders it into a conventional file you can download and share anywhere.

Exports run as **background jobs**: you queue them, keep working, and download the file when the job completes.

## The export wizard

Every export starts in the **export wizard**, a dialog that walks you through **Works → Format → Options → Confirm**. Open it from wherever you are:

| Where | How | The wizard starts with |
|---|---|---|
| **My Works** | **Export…** in the header | Choosing works (ticked works are preselected) |
| A work card | **More actions** (⋮) → **Export…** | Choosing the format for that work |
| **My Works**, several works ticked | **Export…** in the bulk action bar | Choosing the format for the ticked works |
| The editor | **Export** next to **Publish** (save your changes first), or **Export Work…** in the command palette (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>K</kbd>) | Choosing the format for the open work |
| Backstage (Characters, Graph, Preview, …) | The download icon in the header | Choosing the format for the work |
| **Exports** screen | **New export**, or a **Quick start** preset | Choosing works (a preset also sets the format and options) |

<Steps>
  <Step title="Works">
    Search and tick one or more works (**Select all** ticks every work in the list). Each work becomes its own export job.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Format">
    Pick **PDF**, **PowerPoint**, **Video**, **Archive (.zip)**, or **Package with player (.zip)**. Presets below the cards jump straight to their options.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Options">
    Set the format's options (below). An **estimated size** warns before very large exports. **"Save these options as…"** stores the settings as your own preset.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm">
    Check the summary — it also shows how many of your 5 concurrent and 50 daily exports are in use — and click **Start export**. The wizard lists which works were queued; **Go to Exports** takes you to the job list.
  </Step>
</Steps>

You can go **Back** to any earlier step, or click a finished step in the step list.

## The Exports screen

The **Exports** screen lists your export jobs. Open it from the **user menu** (your avatar in the top-right corner) → **Exports**, or with **Go to Exports** at the end of the wizard. **New export** opens the wizard; the **Quick start** row holds the presets. The **← Works** link left of the title takes you back to your works list.

### Format options

#### PDF options

| Option | Choices | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Image quality** | Low (screen, ~96 dpi) / Medium (~190 dpi) / High (print, 300 dpi) | Resolution of the artwork in the PDF. Lettering and balloon outlines stay sharp at every level. High is for professional print and gives the largest file |
| **Title and author** | on/off | Puts the work's title, creators and description into the PDF's document properties |

#### PowerPoint options

| Option | Choices | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Slide Layout** | Standard (4:3) / Widescreen (16:9) | |
| **Include speaker notes** | on/off | |
| **Enable slide animations** | on/off | Animations increase the file size |

#### Video options

| Option | Choices | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Video Resolution** | 720p HD / 1080p Full HD / 4K Ultra HD | 1080p is roughly twice the size of 720p; 4K is much larger and slowest to render |
| **Frame Rate** | 24 fps (Cinematic) / 30 fps (Standard) / 60 fps (Smooth, ~2× size) | The video is rendered at the chosen rate (before 27 September 2026 every video came out at 30 fps) |
| **Include panel audio (voice-over, sound effects, music)** | on/off, plus **Voice-over** / **Sound effects** / **Music & ambience** | Mixes the work's panel audio into the video — see [Soundtrack](#soundtrack). The three role toggles appear when the option is on; all are on by default |

#### Package with player options

See [Package with player](#package-with-player) below.

The options step shows an **estimated size** ("~ a few MB" up to "~ several hundred MB+") and warns when a combination — such as 4K at 60 fps — will be very large and slow.

### Presets

Recurring setups can be saved as one-click **presets**. Four are built in:

- **Email-friendly PDF** — low image quality (~96 dpi), no document properties
- **16:9 deck + notes** — widescreen PowerPoint with speaker notes
- **1080p social clip** — 1080p / 30 fps video with audio
- **Self-hosted site** — package with player, cover and toolbar on

Type a name into **"Save these options as…"** on the wizard's options step to add your own; custom presets can be deleted with their **×** button (in the wizard or the **Quick start** row).

## Archive export

The **Archive (.zip)** format is different from the presentation formats above: instead of rendering the work for readers, it packages the work itself for **transfer and backup**. One archive contains:

- The work's manifest covering **all page formats** — chapters, pages, panels, speech bubbles, hotspots, characters, extras, variables, and settings (including the [preload setting](/cms/editor/layout#the-performance-tab))
- **Every asset file** — originals plus all generated variants (responsive sizes, optimized versions, video renditions, posters); nothing is re-transcoded on import
- The **audio of every speech bubble** — uploaded voice lines and generated [TTS](/cms/editor/speech-bubbles#audio-and-tts) output per language, files included, so the imported copy speaks as the original does
- Your [asset library folders](/cms/assets) and all [translations](/cms/localization)
- Every [paywall rule](/cms/monetization#paywall-rules) — live rules and inactive drafts alike, with prices, tiers, age limit, and preview count. On import they arrive **disabled**, so nothing is sold on the target before you have reviewed it (see [Importing a work archive](/cms/import#importing-a-work-archive)).

The archive's `manifest.json` uses the short **friendly panel ids** (`ch1-p022`) instead of the CMS's internal database ids, and every asset reference points at its file inside the zip (`assets/<asset-id>/w1280.webp`). An unzipped archive is therefore a complete, playable bundle: put the folder on any static web server and load `manifest.json` in the [PanelWave Player](/player/quickstart-embed) by URL.

Archives are created in the export wizard with the **Archive (.zip)** card — there are no options, the options step only summarises what the zip contains — and appear as normal jobs with a download link. Import one on any PanelWave system — another account, another server, or the same one to duplicate a work — via [Import Archive](/cms/import#importing-a-work-archive).

<Callout kind="info">
  Deliberately **not** included: products and reader entitlements, publish history, releases and review links, analytics, and community data (comments, likes). Review the paywall rules, then republish on the target system.
</Callout>

## Package with player

**Package with player (.zip)** makes a self-contained website of one work: the work, its media, and the [PanelWave Player](/player/overview) in an `index.html`. Unzip it and open `index.html` to read the work on your computer, or upload the folder to your own web space by FTP or SFTP. Step by step: [Self-Host Your Graphic Novel](/guides/self-hosting).

| Option | Default | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Start language** | Work default | Readers can still switch languages |
| **Open on the cover** | on | |
| **Show the reader toolbar** | on | |
| **Include original files** | off | Adds your original uploads next to the web versions; much larger zip |
| **Page title** / **Description** | the work's title and description | Per work; used by browser tabs, search engines and link previews |

A self-hosted package is always fully readable — **paywall rules and analytics are not included**. The **Fonts** panel on the options step lists the work's fonts: the open-licensed balloon fonts travel with the player, while **Ames Pro** (Purastik Graphics) is licensed for PanelWave's own websites only and is left out; the panel explains how to license and add it, or **Change balloon font…** opens **Work properties → Typography**.

## Tracking jobs and downloading

The **Export Jobs** list shows every job with filters for **status** (Queued, Processing, Completed, Failed) and **type** (PDF, PowerPoint, Video, Archive, Player package). While one or more jobs are running, a floating **Active Exports** panel stays in the corner of the screen and updates progress live.

Each job card shows the work title, format, when it was created, a status badge, and — while processing — a progress bar with the current step (for example the page being rendered). While a listed job is queued or processing, the Exports page refreshes itself every few seconds, so the bar moves without reloading the page; a queued job says it is waiting for the export service. Depending on status you can:

- **Download** — completed jobs show the file size and a green **Download** button. When the export has something to tell you — fonts you need to license, links that need an internet connection, files that were missing — the card shows **"N notes about this export"**; click it to read them. The file is named after the work and the export time, for example `TARMAC-Pilot_2026-09-30_18-45.zip` (letters, digits and hyphens only; the time is Central European time). Jobs created before 2026-09-30 keep the name `export.<ext>`.
- **Retry** — failed jobs show the error message. If the export service is unavailable when you start an export, the job fails right away with that reason — it no longer waits in **Queued** or counts against your limit of 5 running exports. The wizard's result step lists such failures per work; **Retry** re-queues after a short cancellable countdown, and **"Retry with options…"** reopens the export wizard on its options step, prefilled with that job's settings, so you can adjust before retrying.
- **Cancel** — stop a queued or processing job.
- **Details** — expand the card for the job ID, timestamps, and processing time.

<Callout kind="info">
  Download links are temporary: completed jobs show an **"Expires …"** date. Download your file before it expires, or simply run the export again.
</Callout>

### Export limits

You can run up to **5 exports at the same time** and **50 per day**; a job beyond that is refused with a message saying which limit was hit. Works that became **read-only** because your team is over its plan limit can still be exported (and archived) — only editing is blocked.

## What gets exported

Exports render the work's current state at the time the job runs; hidden layers are skipped. Content that is hidden from readers stays hidden in exports too — for example, [extras](/cms/extras) marked **Draft (hidden from readers)** are excluded, exactly as they are from the published manifest.

### PDF

The PDF is your work's **DIN A4 portrait page view**, lettered: one PDF page (210 × 297 mm) for every page you laid out in the A4 portrait format, in reading order. Each page is rendered by the same player your readers use, so it matches page view exactly:

- **Layout** — panels where you placed them on the page, with their size, rotation and crop.
- **Page colour** — the page's **Background colour** from its page properties, else the work's default page colour (white unless you changed it).
- **Speech balloons** — real balloons with their shape, tail and lettering font (including Ames Pro), styled like in the editor, in the work's default language.
- **Video panels** — the [print still](/cms/assets#choosing-the-print-still-videos) you chose for the video, or its poster frame if you did not choose one. A video that has neither yet (still processing) is left out, and the job lists it under **Warnings**.

Pages in other formats (phone, tablet, landscape, …) are not part of the PDF. A work without any A4 portrait page cannot be exported to PDF: the CMS tells you to lay out A4 portrait pages first.

### PowerPoint

Every panel gets its own slide, drawn from its visible layers bottom to top:

- **Artwork** — every visible image layer, whether it was uploaded, placed from the library, or created by [Page Import](/cms/page-import). In PowerPoint the images are embedded in the deck, so it opens offline.
- **Video layers** — as one still image: the [print still](/cms/assets#choosing-the-print-still-videos) you chose for that video, or its poster frame if you did not choose one. A video that has neither yet (still processing) is left out, and the job lists it under **Warnings**.
- **Text layers** — in the work's default language.
- **Speech bubbles** — as simple text boxes at the bubble's position, in the default language and prefixed with the speaking [character's](/cms/characters) name (*"Mara: Run!"*). Balloon shapes and tails are not drawn.

### Video

The video export plays the work panel by panel:

- A panel with a visible **video layer** contributes that clip (the topmost one, if there are several); any other panel contributes its topmost visible image as a still. See [Video Panels](/cms/editor/video-panels).
- Each panel is on screen for its **Display Duration** (see [Panel duration and video length](/cms/editor/video-panels#panel-duration-and-video-length)); without one, a video panel runs for its clip's length and a still for 5 seconds (always between 0.5 and 120 seconds). A clip shorter than its panel loops, a longer one is cut.
- Every segment is fitted into the chosen resolution without distortion (letterboxed where the aspect ratios differ).
- The clips' own sound is not carried over. With **Include panel audio** on, the video gets a soundtrack from the work's audio instead (below).

#### Soundtrack

With **Include panel audio (voice-over, sound effects, music)** on, the video carries the audio a reader would hear while moving through the panels at the video's pace:

| Audio | Starts | Stops |
|---|---|---|
| A panel's [sound effect](/cms/editor/audio#sound-effects-on-panels) or audio layer | When its panel starts, plus its **Starts after** delay | When the file ends or the panel ends, whichever comes first. It plays again on every panel that uses it; muted sounds are left out |
| The same, set to **Loop** | When its panel starts, plus its Start Time | Keeps looping across consecutive panels that use the same file, and stops at the end of the last of them |
| A [timeline audio track](/cms/editor/audio#timeline-audio-tracks) | At the chapter's start plus the track's start time on the timeline | After its duration (without one, the file's length), and never later than the chapter's end — a **Whole work** track keeps playing to the end of the video. Volume, fades, speed and loop are applied; muted tracks are left out |

The role toggles narrow the mix:

- **Voice-over** — voice-over tracks.
- **Sound effects** — sound effects, including UI sounds.
- **Music & ambience** — music and ambience, plus every track and audio layer without a role.

Also good to know:

- Panel audio that only plays under a condition (`visibleIf`) is skipped — an export has no reader variables to evaluate.
- Files that are missing or can't be decoded are skipped (the export job records a warning for each). If nothing playable is left, the video is exported **silent** — audio never makes an export fail.
- Before 27 September 2026 this option was called **Include audio narration** and laid the work's first audio asset under the whole video. Saved presets and queued jobs keep working and now get the panel-audio mix.

The interactive **manifest** itself (the `panelwave.json` file that drives the [PanelWave Player](/player/overview)) is not produced here — it is built and deployed by the [publish flow](/cms/publishing), where each release's manifest can also be downloaded as JSON from the release history.
