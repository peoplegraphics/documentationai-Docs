---
title: Monetization
description: Configure paywall rules, products, price points, and coupons in the PanelWave CMS — and test what free and paying readers will see.
---

PanelWave lets you decide **what is free and what is paid** in a work. Monetization in the CMS has three parts:

- **Paywall rules** — define *which content is gated* and *what unlocks it* (a subscription, a one-time purchase, or age verification).
- **Products and coupons** — define *what readers can buy*: subscription tiers and one-time purchases, with multi-currency price points and discount codes.
- **The Entitlement lab** — test the whole setup from a reader's point of view, persona by persona, before anything goes live.

For the underlying concepts see [Monetization concepts](/concepts/monetization) and the [paywall section of the format](/schema/paywall).

<Callout kind="alert">
  **Current limitations, honestly:** reader checkout and real payment processing are not live yet — payment-provider connections exist in the product editor but stay disabled until they are configured, and reader access is currently *simulated* for testing rather than driven by real purchases. You can fully configure rules, products, and coupons today; charging readers comes with a later release.
</Callout>

## Paywall rules

Open a work's Backstage and choose **Paywall** in the **Ship** group. The page has two tabs, **Rules** and **Products**, and three zones: the **rules list** on the left, the **content map** in the middle and the **rule inspector** on the right (a drawer on narrower screens; <kbd>]</kbd> toggles it, <kbd>[</kbd> the list, <kbd>?</kbd> the Guide rail).

### The content map

The map shows your gating **on the artwork**: every chapter is a row of panel **thumbnails** in reading order, each tinted and marked **free** (open lock), **preview** (eye — free preview before the paywall) or **gated** (lock, artwork dimmed). Hover a panel to see *which* rule decides it and why (*"The Deal (page 3) — gated by Premium from ch. 3: chapter rule"*). A chapter gated by a chapter rule carries a lock chip in its header (click it to open the rule). An **Extras** row at the bottom shows every bonus item, locked or not. A legend counts *N free · N preview · N gated* for the reader you are looking at.

The **Reader** selector above the map re-tints everything instantly for a different reader: **anonymous**, a **subscriber** of any tier your products sell, a reader who **bought** a product, and an **Age-verified** checkbox. A panel inside the free preview is tinted *preview* only for a reader who could not open it otherwise — a subscriber simply sees it free. **Open the Preview page as this reader** hands the same reader to the [Preview](/cms/preview).

The map header also shows the last 30 days of **paywall views · unlock clicks · conversions** once readers have hit your paywall (*"no paywall hits yet"* until then); the [Analytics](/cms/analytics) board has the same numbers per rule.

### The rules list and health

The list on the left shows every rule with its scope, how many panels and extras it covers and an **Active** switch; **+ New rule** starts one. Under the rules, **Health** flags common mistakes for the anonymous visitor — if **every** panel is gated ("readers get no free preview"), if a rule targets panels that no longer exist, if an active **Chapter** or **Extras Item** rule's target is missing, deleted or (for extras) still a draft — such a rule is left out of the published work, so that content would ship free (preflight reports it as [`PAYWALL_TARGET_MISSING`](/cms/validation-codes#paywall-target-missing)) — and a summary of your products with prices and Stripe state.

### Creating and editing a rule

A rule opens in the inspector. Paywall edits are deliberate, so there is **no autosave**: change what you like, read the diff summary, then click **Save rule** (or **Create rule**); **Revert** restores the stored rule. The fields:

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| **Rule Name** | A label such as "Premium Subscription" |
| **Scope** | **Work Level**, **Chapter**, **Panel Level**, or **Extras Item** |
| **Chapter** / **Extras Item** | Shown for those scopes: pick the chapter or extras item the rule gates. Saving without a target is refused (*"Pick the chapter this rule gates."*) |
| **Target panels** | For **Panel Level** rules: the gated panels as thumbnail chips (× removes one). **Use selection** replaces them with the panels selected on the map, **Add** merges the selection in, **Show on map** highlights them |
| **Requires** | **Subscription**, **One-Time Purchase**, **Age Verification**, or **Free Access** |
| **Subscription tiers** | For subscription rules: the tiers your [products](#products-price-points-and-coupons) sell (basic, premium, pro, or a custom tier) |
| **Price / Currency** | For purchase rules: the price in USD, EUR, GBP, or JPY (when empty, the first unlocking product's price is published) |
| **Unlocking products** | For purchase rules: readers who bought any one of the checked products unlock it; none checked means any purchase does. Each product row shows its price, interval and Stripe state. The player offers a Buy option for each checked product, with that product's name and price |
| **Minimum Age** | Required for **Age Verification** rules; optional on every other rule, where it adds an age check on top of the subscription or purchase |
| **Preview Panel Count** | For work and chapter rules: how many panels readers can view before the paywall |
| **Description** | Optional note about the rule |
| **Active** | Whether the rule is enforced |

Under the form the inspector tells you what the rule does before you save it:

- a **diff summary** — *"Affects 24 panels, 2 extras · changes access for 6 panels · unsaved"*;
- **Affects** — the affected panels as thumbnails;
- a **Reader outcome** table — one row per reader persona (anonymous, every tier, every product, an age-verified visitor when a rule needs an age) with how many panels that reader sees free, as preview or locked, and a verdict (*full*, *preview 5*, *locked*);
- the rule's own paywall views and conversions, created/updated, **Duplicate** and **Delete**.

### Applying rules to panels on the map

Panel-level rules are targeted on the map itself. **Click** a panel to select it, <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>/<kbd>⌘</kbd>-click to add or remove one, <kbd>Shift</kbd>-click for a range in reading order, drag on empty map space to **lasso** several, click a chapter title to select its panels, <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>A</kbd> for everything, <kbd>Esc</kbd> to clear. The selection bar then offers **Apply rule ▾** (every panel rule with its coverage, or **New panel rule from selection**), **Remove from rules** and **Preview here**. You can also **drag a panel rule** from the list onto a panel or onto a chapter header. Every apply or remove is saved immediately. Work, chapter and extras rules do not take individual panels — the map says so if you try.

The editor's structure tree (**Set Paywall** in a panel, page or chapter's context menu) and the bulk operations dialog open this page with those panels preselected, ready for **Apply rule**.

### Paywall screen preview

**Paywall screen preview** under the map embeds the real player, navigated to the first selected gated panel (or the first gated panel for the chosen reader), with that reader's entitlements. You see the actual lock overlay with your products and prices; change the reader and the overlay closes the moment the reader passes.

How each scope gates content:

- **Work Level** — the whole work; the free preview counts from the first panel.
- **Chapter** — only the picked chapter; its free preview counts panels *within that chapter*, and for those panels a chapter rule takes precedence over a work rule.
- **Panel Level** — the listed panels.
- **Extras Item** — only the picked extras item (for example a bonus gallery), never story panels.

With a **Minimum Age** on a subscription or purchase rule, readers need both: the player asks for their age first, then shows the paywall until they subscribe or buy. Age requirements add up across rules: every rule that applies to a panel and names a minimum age must be met, so a chapter's free preview or a **Free Access** panel rule never lets readers skip a work-wide age gate (the gate asks for the highest age that applies). The preview and the simulator below follow the same rule. Rules created before this release with the old **Global (All Works)** scope behave as **Work Level** rules and are labelled *Work Level (legacy "global")*.

<Callout kind="tip">
  Always leave a free preview. The **Preview Panel Count** is what hooks readers before they hit the paywall — the content map warns you if nothing at all is free.
</Callout>

### Rules in moved or copied works

A [work archive](/cms/export#archive-export) carries all of the work's paywall rules, including inactive drafts. When the archive is [imported](/cms/import#paywall-rules-arrive-disabled) — on another system, in another account, or as a copy — every rule arrives **deactivated**, with its settings intact and a note in its **Description** saying when it was imported and whether it was live in the source work. Check each rule, then **Activate** the ones you want. Products and coupons are not part of an archive; set them up on the target system.

## Products, price points, and coupons

The **Products** tab of the Paywall page is where you define what readers can buy. Products are **shared by all works of your team** — the catalogue is the same on every work's Paywall page — and only team owners and admins can change them.

### Products

The tab is a table: name · type · **tier** · price with **regional price chips** · interval · **Active** switch · **Featured** star · **Stripe** state (synced, syncing, out of date, archived, or an error with its message) · **sold** (how many readers bought it) · updated. Sort by any column, filter by search, type and status; **Move up / Move down**, **Sync now**, **Duplicate** and **Delete** sit in the row's actions. A line under the title shows whether your team's Stripe connection is enabled, in test or live mode, and whether its webhook is configured (without the webhook, purchases could not grant access).

Click **+ New product** (or <kbd>n</kbd>) or select a row to work in the inspector, with an explicit **Save product** / **Create product** and **Revert**:

- **Product name**, **Type** — a **Subscription** (with a **Monthly**, **Yearly**, or **Lifetime** billing interval) or a **One-Time Purchase** —, **Description**, and a **Features** list (the selling points shown to readers — at least one is required).
- **Entitlement tier** — **basic**, **premium**, **pro**, a custom name, or none. This is what subscription [paywall rules](#creating-and-editing-a-rule) check, and what [gated extras](/cms/extras#the-inspector) ask for.
- A default **Price** and **Currency** (USD, EUR, GBP, JPY, CAD, AUD), and **Regional prices** — a table of currency- or region-specific prices that override the default. A region-specific price wins over a currency-wide one, which wins over the default. Raising the price of an existing product asks for confirmation, since renewals are affected.
- Payment-provider ids (Stripe product/price, PayPal SKU/plan — collapsed), **Active** and **Featured**, and **Sync now** for Stripe.
- The product's **coupons** as a sub-list, with **New for this product**.

With nothing selected, the inspector shows the **revenue estimate** — a deliberately rough model: monthly readers (preset to your team's readers of the last 30 days, editable) × a conversion rate, split across your active products — and **Prices by region**, which tells you what a reader in, say, Germany or Japan would be charged; a **Wero-ready** chip marks products that have a EUR price.

### Coupons

The **Coupons** list under the table manages discount codes readers will be able to redeem at checkout:

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| **Code** | The redemption code (letters, numbers, hyphens, underscores — e.g. `SAVE25`) |
| **Type** | **Percentage** or **Fixed amount** (fixed amounts have a currency) |
| **Expires** | Optional expiry date (empty = never expires) |
| **Max redemptions** | Optional usage cap (empty = unlimited) |
| **Applies to** | Specific products, or every product if none are selected |
| **Active** | Whether the coupon is redeemable |

Each coupon shows its status (**Active**, **Inactive**, **Expired**, or **Used up**) and usage count, and a **Preview** action calculates the coupon's exact effect on each active product's price — including why it *doesn't* apply (wrong currency, product not covered, expired, and so on).

## Simulating reader access

The **Entitlement** page (Backstage → **Ship** → Entitlement) is a lab for answering "who sees *what*?" before a work is published — no real reader accounts are involved. It is always on: it evaluates the work's **own rules** over its chapters and panels in reading order and the extras items the rules name, with the same logic as the [player](/player/paywall-entitlement#how-rules-are-evaluated), for a set of **personas** built from your **real product catalogue**.

The page has three zones: **personas** on the left, the **outcome matrix** in the middle, the **live player** on the right (<kbd>[</kbd>, <kbd>]</kbd> and <kbd>?</kbd> as on every Backstage tool).

### Personas

The built-in personas cover the readers that matter: **Anonymous** and **Free reader** (age unknown, unverified), one **subscriber per tier** your products sell, one **buyer per one-time product**, a **Teen (15)** and an **Adult, verified (25)**. Built-ins are read-only; **Duplicate** one to get an editable custom persona, or add one with **+ persona** (<kbd>n</kbd>). A custom persona has a name, a subscription (tier, with its product's price, and an optional *subscription ends* date — an expired subscription counts as none), purchases picked from the one-time catalogue, an age and whether it is **age verified**. Custom personas are remembered per work in your browser; **Export / Import JSON** moves them around. The eye button hides a persona from the matrix.

### The outcome matrix

Rows are your chapters — expand one to see its panels with thumbnails — plus an **Extras** row; columns are the visible personas. Each cell is **○ free** (readable), **◐ preview** (readable only because of a rule's free-preview length), **● locked** (hover for the reason and the rule) or **◉ mixed** for a chapter with both free and locked panels (*3/8 locked*). Every column header sums the persona up — *"sees 8/56 panels, 2 preview, 1 extra locked"* — and the same lines are listed under the table. On a wide screen the whole monetization of a work fits on one screen; **Export CSV** writes it out, one column per persona.

Clicking a cell selects its persona and opens that panel in the player.

### The live player

The inspector embeds the real player in a phone frame, started at the selected persona's **first locked panel** (or the cell you clicked), so you see the **actual paywall overlay** with the manifest's products and prices — not a mock-up. The caption names the panel and the reason it is locked.

- **Open in Preview →** opens the [Preview](/cms/preview) page as this persona (the stage shows a *"Previewing as …"* badge; the persona's age travels along), so you can read the work exactly as that reader would.
- **Copy scenario link** copies a link to this lab with the persona encoded; whoever opens it gets the persona in their own custom list, selected.

<Callout kind="info">
  **Simulated vs. enforced.** Subscription and purchase rules are enforced on the server for the [public reader](/guides/publishing) — locked panels never leave it. **Age gates are checked by the player only**; nothing on the server verifies an age. The matrix uses a persona's real age, while the player only knows "age verified, yes or no", so the live player and the Preview link treat a persona as age-verified only when it clears every age gate of the work — the inspector says so when the two differ. A custom tier (say *gold*) is simulated in the matrix but unknown to the player, which understands basic, premium and pro.
</Callout>

**Real grants** — granting a test reader access by email and listing the readers who bought a work — is shown as a placeholder; it arrives with reader accounts and checkout.

## How the paywall reaches readers

Paywall rules travel with your work: publishing writes them into the [manifest](/concepts/manifest) as schema-valid [`paywall.rules`](/schema/paywall) — Chapter, Extras Item and Panel rules carry their target as `refId`, a purchase rule lists all its unlocking products in `requiredProductIds` (format 1.6 — the player offers one **Buy** option per product) and also names the first of them in `requireEntitlement` for older players; each referenced product's name, description and default price are exported in `paywall.products`, so every Buy option shows the product's own name and price, subscription rules list their `subscriptionTiers`, and a minimum age is written as `minimumAge` / `ageGate`. Inactive rules and rules with a missing target are left out. The [PanelWave Player](/player/paywall-entitlement) enforces them when readers hit gated content — showing the lock overlay and checking the reader's entitlements.

On the [public reader](/guides/publishing) the paid parts never leave the server: for an anonymous reader, panels behind a subscription or purchase rule are reduced to a placeholder marked [`"x-locked": true`](/schema/extensions) (title and duration only — no artwork, balloons or audio), and locked extras keep only their title. Readers see a lock in their place; in page view they can click it to open the paywall for that panel. Age gates stay in the reader, since an age is self-declared and the server cannot verify it. [Review links](/guides/publishing#review-links) show the full work, but still ask for the age. Purchasing itself will happen on the reading site through hosted payment pages, not inside the CMS.
