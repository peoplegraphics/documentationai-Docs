PanelWave is sold two ways, because authoring a graphic novel and running a catalogue are different jobs. A **Work Pass** is a one-time purchase for one book. A **subscription** (Creator or Studio) is for people who keep publishing. Publishers get a negotiated agreement built around their titles.

<Callout kind="info">
  **This page explains what each plan is *for*, not what it costs.** Current prices, exact limits, and allowances live on the [pricing page at panelwave.org](https://panelwave.org/pricing) — the single place they are kept up to date.
</Callout>

## Two ways to pay

The dividing line is simple, and every limit follows from it:

- **One-time value → one-time payment.** Authoring a work ends when the work ships. A Work Pass buys that: the editor, the publication slot, and a **fixed bundle** of exports and voice-generation minutes, used whenever you like.
- **Continuing value → continuing payment.** Allowances that **refill every month**, extra team seats, a staging environment, review workflows, and long analytics retention are things you consume for as long as you keep working. Those are subscription-only, and no Work Pass includes them.

That rule is why a Work Pass never gets a second seat and never gets a monthly allowance, no matter how many passes you stack.

## Free

A real publication, not a trial. You get one complete work with enough panels for a full-length European-format graphic novel, two locales, basic monetization, and the whole editor — speech bubbles, hotspots, branching, variables, audio. Exports carry a watermark and analytics use a short retention window.

Free is where you find out whether the medium suits your story. Nothing you make on it is trapped: see [Export](/cms/export).

## Work Pass

A one-time purchase that unlocks **one additional publication permanently**. No subscription, no expiry, no renewal date.

- **Stackable** — buy one per book. Two passes, two extra publications.
- **Full authoring** — the complete editor with no panel cap, more locales than Free, and the full paywall for charging your readers.
- **A one-time credit bundle** — a fixed number of exports and voice-generation minutes that do not refill.
- **Hosting inside a fair-use quota**, per work.

It exists for the creator with one story to tell, who should not have to rent a tool forever to publish it once.

**A Work Pass does not include:** team seats, a staging environment, full review workflows, long analytics retention, or refilling monthly allowances.

<Callout kind="tip">
  Passes stack, but they do not become a subscription. Once you are publishing regularly, Creator costs less than buying pass after pass — the app tells you when you reach that point, and the arithmetic is on the [pricing page](https://panelwave.org/pricing).
</Callout>

## Creator

The subscription for someone building a catalogue on their own. It adds what a pass deliberately leaves out:

- **Many publications** under one account instead of one per purchase.
- **Allowances that refill monthly** — exports and voice generation, every month, rather than once.
- **Longer analytics retention**, so you can see how readers move through a work over a season rather than a week.
- **A production environment** alongside your working one.
- **A small number of team seats**, with a couple more available as an add-on. Beyond that, the plan is Studio — collaboration at scale is what Studio is for.

## Studio

The subscription for a team working on the same books at the same time. On top of everything Creator has:

- **Team seats** in the dozen-plus range, with roles and permissions.
- **A staging environment** as well as development and production, so work in review never touches what readers see.
- **Full review workflows** — the branching/approval process a team needs when more than one person can change a page.
- **Long analytics retention**, measured in years.
- **A custom domain** for your published works, and **SSO/OIDC** available for teams that need it.
- **Priority support.**

If you are more than a handful of people, the seat count is usually what decides this, not the feature list.

## Publisher / Enterprise

A negotiated agreement rather than a self-serve plan, structured in two parts because publishers budget per title: an **annual platform fee** plus a **per-title fee** for each published book.

The platform side is about what a contract provides and a credit card cannot:

- **Contractual SLA**, negotiated data-processing terms, and support for your vendor security review.
- **Deployment choice** — shared cloud, private cloud, or on-premise.
- **White-label publishing** under your own brand rather than PanelWave's.
- **Custom data retention and residency** commitments.
- **Procurement fit** — purchase orders, annual invoicing, a custom contract.
- **Dedicated onboarding and a named account manager.**

Start with a single pilot title and grow with the catalogue. [Contact sales](https://panelwave.org/contact?subject=enterprise) for a quote.

## What happens when you reach a limit

Nothing is deleted and nothing is held hostage.

- **Content stays yours and stays intact.** If you move to a smaller plan, anything above its limits becomes read-only until you upgrade again.
- **Metered usage keeps working, and you pay for what you use.** Storage, bandwidth, exports and voice generation have published overage rates on the [pricing page](https://panelwave.org/pricing).
- **Above a Work Pass's fair-use hosting quota you have three honest exits:** pay the metered overage, move to a subscription, or export everything and host it yourself.

That last option is real, not a formality. The manifest format is open (CC BY 4.0) and the player is MIT-licensed, so a work you export runs anywhere you put it — see [Export](/cms/export) and the [player documentation](/player/overview).

<Callout kind="info">
  **Plans are what *you* pay PanelWave. They are not what your *readers* pay you.** Charging readers — paywall rules, products, price points, coupons — is a separate system, documented in [Monetization](/cms/monetization). PanelWave takes **no revenue share** on your content sales on any plan.
</Callout>
